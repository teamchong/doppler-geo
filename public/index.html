Find Where a Recording Was Made from Airplane Sound

Where was this recorded?

When a propeller plane or helicopter flies overhead, its engine pitch drops as it passes. doppler-geo measures that pitch drop from an audio or video clip and compares it with public flight tracks worldwide to find where the recording was made.

Audio stays in your browser · No GPS, IP lookup, or cookies
Step 1Measure the Pitch Drop
A plane sounds higher-pitched as it approaches and lower-pitched as it flies away. From that pitch change, we calculate how fast the plane was flying and how close it passed.
Step 2Search Flights Worldwide
We check public flight tracks around the world to find every propeller plane or helicopter that was flying at that same speed and distance.
Step 3Pinpoint the Spot on the Map
1 plane gives a shortlist of possible flights worldwide. 2 planes flying past the same spot from different directions cross at 1 exact location on the map.

    What We Found in Real-World Testing Key Limits

    What happens when you test airplane sound geolocation across the whole world with no location hints
    Finding 1Hearing Just 1 Plane Cannot Pinpoint Your Location
    The sound of a single plane only tells you how fast it flew and how close it passed. It cannot tell you which direction or country it was in. Because hundreds of small planes around the world fly at the exact same speed at any moment, 1 plane only gives a long list of hundreds of possible matches worldwide (like Example 2). You must record at least 2 different planes flying past the same spot to lock onto one exact location on the map (like Example 1).
    Finding 2Big Commercial Jets (737, A320, A380) Do Not Work
    Jet airliners make a loud, rushing roar across all frequencies rather than a single steady musical note, and pilots constantly change engine power during climb and landing. This method only works on propeller planes, turboprops, and helicopters whose spinning blades produce a steady hum.
    Finding 3Needs Daytime & an Outdoor Microphone
    Small propeller planes mostly fly during the day in clear weather and stay away from major city airports. Recording indoors or overnight usually catches nothing because very few prop planes fly at night, and windows plus room or computer fan noise drown out distant propeller hum.
